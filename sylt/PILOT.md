# Sylt usability and pilot protocol

## Initial usability sessions

Recruit 6–8 adult beginner/amateur musicians, mixing instruments, voice, desktop use, and narrow-screen use. Obtain recording/research consent separately. Run 30–40 minute sessions with two isolated test accounts and generated media before using participant recordings.

Tasks: explain the product from the welcome screen; sign in and complete optional setup; upload or capture a take; save privately; identify who can access it; replay it from My Practice; find and connect with another test account from Home; share deliberately; comment on a friend's take; inspect public-link warnings; locate removal controls. Include a denied permission and interrupted-upload recovery scenario. Ask participants to use keyboard navigation for one flow.

Observe unassisted completion, save time, audience comprehension, confusion between Home and Friends, and pressure to perform or publish. Suggested launch gates: at least 80% unassisted first-save completion, all participants correctly distinguish private/friends/public access, and no critical access-control or data-loss failure. Investigate accessibility blockers before wider recruitment.

After tasks, ask (1–5): I felt free to choose what to record/share; I felt comfortable saving an imperfect take; I could recognize my progress; encouragement felt supportive; the app made me feel pressured. Ask what caused each answer. These questions operationalize autonomy, identity, competence, relatedness, influence, and emotion; they are not a validated psychological scale.

## Four-week pilot

Recruit a small group of consenting adults after the two-account smoke checks pass. Deploy the app to your own HTTPS domain before inviting participants. Each musician should know at least one other participant. Give a minimal introduction and no mandatory posting schedule or streak incentive.

Use consented weekly self-report plus participant-authorized review of their own records. Do not silently collect private recordings or comments for research. Record first-save completion, active recording days per week, week-two return, optional share rate, and the proportion of friend posts receiving encouragement. Record sample size and missing data with every rate. Compare week one with week four confidence, autonomy, perceived support, and pressure ratings; use interviews to interpret results.

Check weekly for upload failures, unavailable media, duplicate writes after retries, confusing audience labels, harassment reports, and device/browser problems. Escalate privacy failures immediately and pause affected sharing. Do not claim causal habit improvement or dopamine effects from this uncontrolled pilot.

## Manual production smoke checks

1. Real account A logs in and saves a private recording. Account B and a logged-out browser cannot fetch its metadata or media.
2. A requests B; B accepts in Home's Friends panel. A shares a new take. B can play and comment; unrelated account C cannot.
3. A creates a public copy. On your HTTPS domain, a logged-out browser can play only that selected share; private history and comments stay unavailable.
4. Remove the public share. Reload its URL from another browser; the share is unavailable. The original private practice remains playable.
5. Verify microphone/video capture and permission denial on Safari, Chrome, iOS Safari, and Android Chrome, including the five-minute cap.
6. Verify keyboard focus, screen-reader announcements, reduced motion, mobile panel scrolling, feed position retention, and retry recovery.

These sessions require real participants and accounts. The protocol is prepared; recruitment and the four-week study have not been conducted.
